## The single most important change

**The cloud publishes its own feed, and it needs no secret.** Before today
the nightly sweep ran the whole pipeline, found real listings, and buried
every one in a 7-day GitHub artifact no browser could read. The live feed
only refreshed when David's Mac happened to run.

`.github/workflows/sanctioned-cloud-sweep.yml` now force-pushes to an orphan
`feed` branch, served at
`raw.githubusercontent.com/omgitsthedm/vera-apartment-search/feed/{public,archive,meta}.json`
with `access-control-allow-origin: *`, using only the automatic
`GITHUB_TOKEN`.

**Do NOT create a Netlify PAT for VERA.** Settled; see `SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md`.

## What was wrong, and is not any more

- **Five of 42 target neighbourhoods could never match.** The target list
  was passed through `canonical_text()`, the STREET-ADDRESS normaliser, so
  "East Village" became `e village` and "Upper East Side" `upper e side`.
  The same mangling made `e village` a substring of `middle village`, so
  Queens passed as East Village.
- **Five of nine sources were capped at $2,500** against a $3,000 ceiling.
  Every one of the 23 shortlisted listings sits in that missing band.
- **The craigslist budget was split evenly across five boroughs**, half of
  it on boroughs holding no target neighbourhood. Reallocated 85/15 by
  target borough: in-target listings went 81 → 136 at the same request count.
- **All 19 discovery functions asserted `status: "ok"`.** streeteasy
  published as healthy with zero listings. Status is now derived from each
  run's own query outcomes, history-free.
- **`state/` is gitignored, so the cloud had no memory.** Restored via the
  Actions cache — 14 stores including photo hashes, price history,
  `ai_photo_flags` and `listing_history`. Deliberately the cache and not a
  branch: that data has not passed the public lens and this repo is public.
- **Three enrichment steps had never run in the cloud** (price memory,
  photo fingerprints, landlord portfolios).
- **All three forensic tells accused landlords of their own ordinary
  behaviour** — one owner reusing a photo, a template, or a leasing phone
  across their own buildings scored as a clone, a template scam and a
  contact ring. They now suppress only when both sides have a known owner
  and the owners match.
- **`ownerRead` called every LLC "Corporate."** In NYC that mislabels a
  family with one walk-up. Classification now uses JustFix building count.
- **The owner's portfolio rendered on the Records tab**, one tab away from
  the question it answers.

## Measured and rejected

**Reverse-geocoding craigslist map pins.** 27 of 42 qualified listings are
`partial_address_only` and all carry coordinates, and GeoSearch has a
working reverse endpoint returning a BBL. Tested against 24 records holding
both a full street address and a pin: **the correct house number came back
0 times.** Craigslist offsets pins by design. Full write-up in
`docs/proposals/reverse-geocoding-craigslist-pins.md`.

## The finding that should shape what comes next

Of 37 listings with portfolio data, **34 belong to landlords holding 10+
buildings, 3 hold 3–9, and not one holds 1–2.** VERA's premise is
owner-direct, non-corporate rentals; among the listings it can actually
verify, those are close to absent. The sources currently reachable surface
portfolio operators. That is a sourcing question, not a scoring one.
